# Using your telescope helper

Everything works with the sound off. Both cameras are live all the time: the **finder** (wide
view) and the **telescope** (what the telescope's camera sees). Hold the tablet upright to see both, one
above the other; turn it sideways to see one large, and swipe or tap its name for the other.
Messages appear in the line under the pictures (tap it to see the earlier ones), with anything
that's wrong, like **Finder ✗**, at its right.

**Getting started**

1. Turn on the telescope box. Wait one minute.
2. Open **Astro** on the tablet. The first time at a new place it asks to use the tablet's
   location: tap **Allow**.
3. Check both pictures show stars. If one says **not connected**, check its cable.

**Is it worth going out tonight?**

Open **More**, then **Tonight at each spot** (or the bookmarked web page, which works even when
the telescope box is off). It lists, for each place you set up, what clears the trees tonight,
between what times, and how cloudy it will be.

**A new spot**

Stand where the telescope will go, open **More**, then **Record a treeline**, type a name like
"Back deck", point the back of the tablet at the treetops and tap **Mark** as you turn all the
way around. Tap **Save**.

**Find something**

1. Tap **Go to** and pick from tonight's list (best first; grayed out ones are behind the trees
   or below the horizon right now).
2. Push the telescope the way the big arrow on the finder picture points. The number under it
   is how far there is to go. A dot means you're on it.
3. The **dashed box** on the finder picture is what the telescope sees. Anything inside it is
   in the telescope view.
4. **Names** (bottom corner of the finder picture) writes star names, planets and Go to targets
   beside them. They come from the finder working out where it points, which it does by itself
   every few seconds when it can see stars.

**Focus**

Tap **Focus**. Each picture shows a **focus number**: turn that camera's focus knob slowly and
make the number as high as you can. The line under it shows how many stars it measured and the
best number so far; the little graph shows it going up and down as you turn.

**Take a picture**

1. With the target in the telescope view, tap **Capture**. It works out what's there by itself;
   if it sees more than one thing (a planet in a star cluster), it asks which. Planets and the
   Moon record a short video, with Jupiter's moons in the picture; everything else builds up in
   a small picture in the corner of the telescope picture, away from the target.
2. The telescope picture says how many frames so far and **edge in … s**: how long until the target
   drifts out of view (the telescope doesn't follow the sky).
3. When the edge time gets short, either tap **Stop capture**, or tap **Recenter**: the capture
   pauses, an arrow on the finder picture leads back to the target, and **Resume** carries on
   with the same picture.
4. When it says the picture is ready, tap **Pictures**. Tap a picture to see it large, with
   brighter, darker and zoom buttons.

**Align (only if the dashed box looks wrong)**

Put a bright star, one you can easily see by eye, in the middle of the telescope picture. Tap
**Align** and don't touch the telescope for 10 seconds. The box on the finder picture moves to
where the telescope really points, and **Go to** uses it from then on. If it asks for a
brighter star, pick a brighter one.

**Good to know**

- **STOP** stops whatever is going on: guiding, or a picture being taken.
- Each picture has **−** and **+** (brightness on the tablet only) and **1x/2x/4x** (zoom).
- The screen is red to protect your night vision. **More**, then **Day mode** changes it.
- Put in or took out the Barlow? **More**, then **Barlow in** or **Barlow out**.
- Lost? **More**, then **Sync now**, with the finder pointed at clear sky.
- **More** also shows the details: the time, where it thinks it's pointing, camera settings.
